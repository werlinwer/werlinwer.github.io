Changelog

Everything that changed in the game, update by update, newest first. Each one opens with its highlights.

November 4, 2026

Fixes and lighter long runs

  • Long endless runs got lighter: move effects cost much less to draw, and Lean mode cuts even more when the device struggles.
  • The Super Rod now reels in everything on the ground: Oran Berries, coins, Exp. Candies and the shinies’ Poké Balls too.
  • Forgetting the Metronome item no longer wipes Mew’s Metronome move, and big Pokémon like Chesnaught fit through the Underground’s tunnels.
  • Pressing Esc in a co-op room no longer freezes the site, and on phones the game screen redraws itself if it goes black.
All changes (2)

Long runs

  • Move effects (Heat Wave, rings, spokes, bursts, Arc) and damage numbers are now drawn in batches instead of pixel by pixel: late in a two-hour run, drawing the screen costs 20% to 40% less, with the same look.
  • In Lean mode, the regular horde stops showing poison, burn and slow tints, runners lose their speed lines and Roar of Time’s clock over each foe goes away. Bosses, Alphas and frozen or stunned foes stay marked.
  • Moves that last the whole run (auras, trails, Raging Fury) remembered every foe they had ever hit, and that list only grew. Now it forgets the ones that can already be hit again.
  • While the game is paused and the tab is in the background, it frees its image memory and rebuilds it on return, so the browser closes the tab (and the run) less often.

Fixes

  • The Super Rod pulled in almost everything but left Oran Berries, coins, Exp. Candies and the Poké Balls shinies drop on the ground. Now it pulls in everything except a choice between two or more Mega Stones.
  • Metronome is both a move and an item. Forgetting the item also blocked the move, which stayed stuck at Lv 1. Now one doesn’t affect the other.
  • In the Underground, big Pokémon like Chesnaught couldn’t get through the tunnels to the three round rooms. The tunnels now reach into the rooms, and any Pokémon fits.
  • The ruins turned grey for a moment when they woke up. Now their lit art loads together with the unlit one.
  • In a co-op room, Esc opened and closed the leave dialog endlessly and froze the site. Now Esc leaves at once when you’re alone, and asks first when someone else is there.
  • iPhone: the game screen could go black (only the minimap and the move buttons showing) and the Pokémon stopped walking. Now the screen redraws itself, and a new touch takes over the joystick if the previous finger was lost.
  • Rare sound errors (on iPhone) and lobby art that failed to load no longer interrupt the game.

November 3, 2026

Biomes, maps and Leavanny’s Boutique

  • The map is now a patchwork of nine biomes, Minecraft style: walk and one ends as the next begins, with no loading screen. Four are new: Route 109’s beach, the Power Plant, the Route 111 desert and Mt. Chimney.
  • Every run draws a kind of map (Archipelago, Canyon, Deep Forest, Ruins Field…), and the terrain suits your Pokémon: Water surfs the lakes, Flying glides over water and Ghost slips through trees and rocks.
  • Hidden zones: in some runs, a secret way in shows up on the map with no arrow and no warning. Keep an eye out for rustling leaves and glinting rocks.
  • Co-op rankings: Duos, Trios and Squads get their own boards, by difficulty, today, this week and all time, with a friends filter. Each team shows everyone’s Pokémon.
  • Leavanny’s Boutique opened on the plaza: buy gems (card, Apple Pay, Google Pay or Pix) and dress your Pokémon in hats, a balloon and trails, in every mode (runs, co-op, the Dojo and the Arena). Looks only, never power.
All changes (12)

Biomes on the map

  • Biomes no longer change with the clock: each region of the map has its own, several screens across, and at the border the ground blends little by little. The minimap shows the regions around you. Difficulty and stages still follow the clock, wherever you are.
  • Near the start: Route 1, Viridian Forest and the beach. Further out: Mt. Moon, the Power Plant and the desert. Far away: Mt. Silver, Mt. Chimney and Lavender Tower. Farther still, they all mix.
  • Terrain, breakables and points of interest dress for the biome they stand in: palms and shells on the beach, generators, power poles and drums at the Power Plant, cacti and pyramids in the desert, basalt, burnt trees and hot springs on Mt. Chimney.
  • Eevee leans towards Vaporeon on the beach, Jolteon at the Power Plant and Flareon on Mt. Chimney, Arceus’s Judgment switches plates in each new biome and the world’s attacks gain water spouts, sparks and lava bombs.
  • In co-op, everyone sees the name and hears the music of the biome they stand in; the weather follows the team’s lead.

Kinds of map

  • Archipelago (like the Sevii Islands): lakes and channels everywhere, joined by narrow strips of land. Canyon (like Victory Road): rock walls and gorges with tight passes. Deep Forest (like Viridian Forest): walls of trees forming trails. Ruins Field (like the Ruins of Alph): ancient columns that block enemy shots. Open Plains: open country, with the horde coming from every side.
  • The sign at the start says which types the map is good for, and the pause menu shows the map and how your Pokémon crosses the terrain.

Terrain and types

  • Water-type Pokémon now surf the lakes faster than they walk, leaving a wake. Flying (and Levitate) never gets slowed in water nor slides on ice. Ice skates on ice, faster and without sliding. Ghost slips through trees, bushes, rocks, logs, columns and hedges; mountains, rock walls, towers and walls still stop it. In co-op, each player crosses their own way.

Hidden zones

  • In about one run in three, the way into a hidden zone shows up just ahead of where you’re heading, dressed as scenery. There’s no arrow, no warning and no mark on the minimap: walk up to it to find it, and stand in its way in to enter.
  • In the Hidden Grotto, the grass shakes and an Audino (sometimes a Chansey or a Blissey) pops out, shy and running: defeat it before it dives back in. At the back of the clearing an item lies in the grass: a Poké Ball, a pile of ₽, a Rare Candy or the evolution item your Pokémon is missing.
  • In the Underground, stand at the glints in the walls to dig: Exp. Candy, a Prism Sphere, a Rare Candy, the evolution stone your Pokémon is missing or Old Amber with an egg inside. Every dig cracks the walls, and the Diglett only peek out of their holes for a moment.
  • In about one visit in three, a tuft of grass or a hole starts to sparkle: the Pokémon that comes out is a sure shiny, worth five times the EXP, and leaves a Poké Ball, ₽ and a shot at an egg.
  • The first time you step into each zone brings a Mystery Gift with a look no shop sells: the Hidden Grotto frame (moss green, with leaves in the corners) and the Secret Base wallpaper (earth and spheres, like the Underground’s bases). There’s also a gold medal and a title for finding them all.

Leavanny’s Boutique

  • Leavanny, who sews clothes out of leaves, opened a Boutique on the north-west side of Kecleon’s plaza. Talk to her (or use the Boutique button in Kecleon’s shop) to open the Shop and Look tabs. In the Shop you buy gem packs, with a bonus on the bigger ones, in reais or dollars: card, Apple Pay and Google Pay, plus Pix when paying in reais. Gems stay on your account.
  • Gems buy looks: hats and a balloon your Pokémon wears, plus trails, titles and plates for your name and HP bar. Try before you buy: the preview shows your partner wearing the item. No look changes the game.
  • In the Look tab (also on your Trainer Card) you choose what to wear in each slot. Your look is saved to your account and shows in every mode: runs, the Dojo, Legend mode, co-op and the Arena, where your team and rivals see it too. It fits every Pokémon you can play, Megas included.
  • The Arena also has entrance, knockout and goal effects, and emotes: press B (or tap the smiley), and a win shows its pose on the end screen. Gems also come from playing (the day’s first run of 5 minutes or more, five days of play in the same week, and the Arena, up to a daily limit) and supporting on Patreon, and the Welcome Pack, one per account, brings twice the gems for the smallest pack’s price.

Co-op rankings

  • The Ranking has a Co-op side, next to Standard and Legendary: pick Duos, Trios or Squads, the difficulty and the period. Each team (the same accounts) shows once, with its best run, its time and whether it won or wiped. Tap a trainer to see their profile.
  • Daily co-op challenge: on the Daily Challenge screen, the Co-op tab shows today’s difficulty, the best teams and a Play co-op button that opens a room with the challenge already on. One board per team size, no ribbons.
  • Co-op runs now count for the rankings: a room of one doesn’t, and Arena matches stay on the Arena’s own ranking.

Dojo

  • New Duo shelf on the Dojo’s Moves tab, with every duo move (Fire Pledge, Fusion Flare, Nightmare…). As in a run, you can carry only one at a time.
  • The Dojo’s Form tab now has Mega Evolution: pick the Mega (or none) and press MEGA on the final form. The Mega lasts and recharges as in a run, so the DPS meter shows the real damage.
  • Both Dojo additions come from Lucas-BRT, who sent the code through the game’s GitHub. Thanks, Lucas!

Items

  • Three new items drop from Item Balls, tall grass, berry trees and foes, and work like Exp. Share for other things: Coin Case pulls the ₽, Berry Pouch pulls the Oran Berries and Super Rod pulls the items on the ground. A community suggestion from Discord.

Abilities

  • A move your Ability boosts (Sharpness on slicing moves, Iron Fist on punches, Torrent on Water moves, Adaptability on your own types…) shows the Ability’s icon in its corner: on the screen’s slots, the level-up cards and the pause menu. The move’s card says which Ability powers it.
  • Sharpness (Oshawott, Treecko, Ralts and others) now boosts Aqua Cutter, Liquidation, Trailblaze and Leaf Storm, the evolutions of Water Shuriken, Razor Shell, Leaf Blade and Razor Leaf, and also Sacred Sword and Fury Cutter. Before, evolving a slicing move lost the bonus.
  • The same goes for the other move-kind Abilities: Jaw Lock counts as a bite (Strong Jaw), Superpower, Dynamic Punch, Focus Punch and Meteor Mash as punches (Iron Fist), and Alluring Voice, Burning Jealousy, Giga Drain, Echoed Voice, Snarl and Sparkling Aria as sound moves (Liquid Voice, Punk Rock).
  • Swablu and Altaria swap Cute Charm for Cloud Nine, their Hidden Ability in the main games: rain, sun, sand and snow no longer wear your HP down or make foes’ hits stronger. The world’s attacks still land. An idea from Bluclair on Discord.

Minimap

  • When you see “A shiny Pokémon appeared!”, check the minimap: the shiny is the blinking gold star. Out of the map’s range, it sits on the rim pointing where it is, like the boss and the Alphas. Idea by Zozup on Discord.

Balance

  • Protect and Needle Arm could keep you untouchable forever: with Light Clay and Quick Claw maxed (and a fast Pokémon like Mew or Mewtwo), the bubble lasted longer than the cooldown and came back before it popped. Now the protection lasts at most half the move’s cooldown, so there’s always a window for foes to land a hit. Thanks to Kitiara on Discord for the report.

Fixes

  • A TM armed at the Kecleon Shop comes out of the first boss you defeat, in place of that boss’s own TM (that’s how it reaches the run). The screen, though, guessed who left it from the move: with Iron Head armed, defeating Onix said "Registeel left a TM!", and a shop-only TM (like Pay Day) showed up as found on the ground. Now it says it’s your shop TM.
  • Fly no longer makes your Pokémon invincible forever: with lots of Light Clay and a short cooldown, one landing ran into the next takeoff and nothing could hit you. Now the time in the air (and Dig’s time underground), plus the landing’s protection, covers at most half the cooldown, and Fly has a 2 s minimum cooldown, like Dig. Ordinary builds don’t change. Thanks for the report on Discord!

November 1, 2026

Fixes

  • The game no longer freezes on screen (only the minimap moving) after coming back from another app, or after pressing Back and choosing to stay. If it ever does, pausing and resuming rebuilds the screen.
  • Late in long runs (the Tower, fog, Arceus) the game uses much less image memory, so it doesn’t close by itself on phones. On iPhone, low on memory, the scenery and the plaza no longer throw errors.
  • Starting a run takes much less memory, so phones no longer close the game right at the start. If it closes more than once, Lite mode gets lighter still, and goes back to normal on its own after a few runs without trouble.
  • On a phone held sideways, the pause menu shows all six moves without cutting off the last one (often the one that just evolved).
All changes (1)

Fixes

  • Android (Firefox and others): after pressing Back and choosing to stay, or coming back from another app, the game’s screen could stay frozen while only the minimap moved. The screen is now rebuilt when you come back and when you leave the pause. On iPhone, when the device runs out of memory for images, the game also recovers without errors.
  • On a phone held sideways, the pause menu’s move list cut off the sixth move (often the one that just evolved), so it looked gone. The list’s icons are smaller now and all six moves fit without scrolling.
  • Many phones (iPhone and low-memory Android) closed the game in a run’s first seconds. Now the lobby’s art (the plaza, the Estádio, the Dojo) is freed when you press Play, and the next biome’s scenery and the next stage’s Pokémon load once the run is under way, instead of all at once in its first second.
  • Lite mode: if the game closes again with it on, it gets lighter still (fewer particles, thinner rain and snow, fewer Pokémon loaded ahead). Five long runs without a close step it back a level, and three more turn it off.
  • Late in long runs (the Tower, fog, Arceus, hundreds of foes) the game could close on its own on phones. Now the Pokémon’s flashes and outlines and the fog take far less memory, with nothing changed on screen.

  • Co-op, iPhone and moves: the world waits for whoever picks alone, the screen no longer goes blank after locking the phone, the game plays on silent and Aqua Step lets you open chests.
All changes (2)

Fixes

  • In the Arena, Dragon Tail and Breaking Swipe no longer teleport anyone: they never throw you or your teammates, and a thrown rival is only pushed a little.
  • Moves that carry your Pokémon (Aqua Step, Quick Attack, U-turn, Brave Bird, Rollout…) now stop when it reaches a locked chest, the cave’s treasure chest or the Move Tutor’s tent, as they already did at doors. Before, Aqua Step threw you off the chest with every step and it never finished opening.
  • Focus Punch and Sky Uppercut recharge in a second or two, and every punch froze the game for an instant: it looked like your Pokémon got stunned every second. Now the punch shakes the screen without freezing it.
  • With the Metronome (the item), the moves you fire with the button (U-turn, Quick Attack, Extreme Speed…) no longer go off on their own when the special lands: they wait for your press.
  • Co-op: while you picked a TM, a Poké Ball prize, a cursed item or where your evolution goes, the run went on and foes piled up on top of you; closing the screen, you took all the damage at once. Now, if your teammate has fainted (or is picking too), the world waits for you, as in solo, and coming back from any of those screens gives you 2 seconds of invincibility to get out of the crowd.
  • iPhone: with the game paused, locking the phone for a while could leave the game’s screen blank behind the menu when you came back. Now the frame is drawn again when you come back, and every so often while paused.
  • iPhone: with the silent switch on, the game made no sound at all, music included. Now, with the game’s music on, it plays like a music app (and pauses another app’s music); with Music at 0 or Sound off in Settings, it follows the silent switch and leaves your own music playing.
  • When the browser cleared or couldn’t read the save of a device that stayed signed in, the blank save replaced the account’s and everything was gone (hatched eggs, legendaries, achievements). Now the account merges both and gives your progress back to the device.

Creators page

  • The first videos are by WePlay Z (in Portuguese) and Son Titan Games (in Spanish), and BirdseyeGaming streams on TikTok. Each creator shows up with links to their channels.
  • The Content Creator title and the creator frame (pink and cyan, with play buttons in the corners) arrive as a Mystery Gift.

October 29, 2026

Arena: aimed moves, 3v3, coast maps and teamplay

  • Arena now plays like Pokémon Unite: your basic attack fires on its own, and Move 1, Move 2 and the Unite Move go off when you press Q, E and R (or the on-screen buttons).
  • In a hurry? 3v3 Quick lasts 3 minutes, with two goals per team. And two maps have landed on the coast: the Cove (4v4) and the Reef (3v3), drawn each match or picked in a room with friends.
  • Play as a team: quick chat, map pings, a full scoreboard, surrender votes and "Rematch with team".
  • Fair Play: leaving matches costs points and locks Ranked, and Protection covers a loss. Until November 2 it’s the pre-season, with no RP.
  • On phones, a cluster of buttons for your right thumb: tap to cast, drag to aim, slide back onto the button to cancel.
All changes (6)

Your moves, your aim

  • The basic attack is your Pokémon’s own move (Ember for Charmander, Leech Seed for Bulbasaur…) and aims by itself: at your locked target or the rival your priority picks. In Settings → Controls you choose the aim (assisted or manual) and the priority (closest, lowest HP % or lowest HP).
  • Lock onto a target with a click or a tap on the Pokémon, and switch with Tab. Assisted aim hits your locked target; manual aim goes exactly where the mouse or your finger points.
  • Move 1 comes from the opening draft, Move 2 at level 3, and level-up cards now upgrade those three moves. On evolving, the new form offers two of its moves to take Move 1’s place (first evolution) and Move 2’s (second), at the same level and with the forks already taken.
  • In the Arena you evolve sooner: at levels 5 and 9 in the 4v4, 6 and 9 in the 3v3.
  • Rebindable keyboard and gamepad: Q, E, R and Tab by default; on a gamepad R, ZR and ZL cast and the right stick aims and switches targets (a flick locks the rival it points at). E is still Mega Evolution outside the Arena.
  • Bots cast and aim their moves too. In tall grass, Move 1 and Move 2 still hold; the Unite Move works, but gives you away.
  • Moves with a lasting effect, like Flame Wheel’s trail or Whirlpool’s aura, last 5 seconds each time you cast them.

3v3 Quick in the Glade

  • The Arena has two formats: 4v4 Standard in the Vale (7 minutes) and 3v3 Quick in the Glade (3 minutes). Pick the format on the Arena screen, for practice, the Casual queue or a room with friends.
  • The Glade is a compact map: two short lanes with one goal each per team, no base goal, and a clearing in the middle where Drednaw arrives at 0:40 and Zapdos in the last minute, with double points. EXP counts 1.5×, and there you evolve at levels 6 and 9, so you can reach your final form before the end.
  • 3v3 is Casual only for now, so the season has a single ranking. The quick match against bots is now called Practice.

Two maps on the coast

  • Each format has a second map, on a beach of sand, boardwalks and tidepools: the Cove in 4v4 and the Reef in 3v3. A match draws one of its format’s maps; in a room with friends, the host picks the map or leaves it on Random.
  • The Cove: its lanes bow out to sea, and their middle is far from everything. In the center, Drednaw and Zapdos land on an island you can only reach over the sandbars from each side’s cove and down two long, narrow piers, one from each lane.
  • The Reef: a channel splits the map down the middle, and the two halves only meet on each lane’s narrow bridge (with Wingull nesting on it) and on the reef in the center, joined to the lagoons by causeways. Every crossing is a fight.
  • On the coast, Krabby and Staryu hold the lanes, Wingull sits in their middle and Lapras and Corsola in the lagoons. The tall grass is dune grass there, and hides you just the same.

Arena: playing as a team

  • Quick chat with ready-made lines (Let’s go!, Fall back!, Help!, Take the objective!…) and map pings (Attack here, Danger, On my way, Objective), on the field and on the mini map. Only your team sees them, except "Good game!", which reaches everyone, and each player reads them in their own language. On a computer, T opens the chat and G pings where the mouse points (or Alt+click, or click the mini map); on a phone, use the buttons beside the mini map, or hold the mini map. Quick chat can be muted in Settings.
  • Surrender: from the middle of the match on, the menu has "Surrender". The team votes for 20 seconds and it passes with 2/3 of the team’s trainers (alone with bots, you decide). It counts as a normal loss in Ranked, not as leaving.
  • Full scoreboard: hold the ` key or tap the score to see everyone’s KOs, assists, faints, points, damage dealt and taken, healing and interrupted scores; the end screen shows the same. At the end, "Rematch" plays again with the same room, and in the public queue "Rematch with team" queues again together with the teammates who press it too.

Fair Play and Protection

  • Every account has Fair Play, from 0 to 100, starting at 100. In a Casual or Ranked match with another trainer, leaving before the end takes 15, 60 seconds in a row without playing (no moving, casting or locking a target) or away takes 10, and each match played to the end gives 2 back. Dropping and coming back in time doesn’t count.
  • Below 80, Ranked is locked until you finish a few Casual matches; below 60, the queue also waits longer before searching for your match. The Arena’s Profile shows your Fair Play and tells you when you lost points.
  • Protection: good Ranked matches fill a bar. Full, your next loss costs no ranking points, and the end screen says "Protection used!".
  • A Ranked match now needs trainers on both teams; if they all land on one side, it counts as Casual. Surrendering isn’t leaving: the whole team finishes the match.
  • Pre-season: until November 2, Ranked matches go to your history with no RP, and the queue, end-of-match and Profile screens say so. The ranking starts with Season 1.

Fixes

  • On iPhone, the game screen could go black in long runs (iOS wipes the stored drawings when memory runs low or the app goes to the background). Now the game notices and redraws everything within a few seconds.
  • When Arceus breaks free because legendaries are still missing, the notice now names which ones (and its card on the Legends tab lists them all), instead of only how many.
  • Pyro Ball has its own look now: a bigger fireball spinning with its charred patches, trailing embers over its shadow on the ground, so it no longer looks like Ember.
  • On a computer, clicking the lobby’s background no longer sends your Pokémon walking off: with the mouse, it walks in Explore (or with the movement keys). On phones, tapping the grass still takes it for a walk.
  • The maze’s chest now shows open once taken (it stayed closed). In co-op, the treasures of the maze, the camp, the cottage and the ruins already show open: there they’re just scenery and never opened.
  • Fullscreen in Settings → Game, on Android and computers (on iPhone, install the game to your Home Screen).
  • Arena and co-op on lower-end phones: when the fight gets heavy (Zapdos in the middle with everyone around it), the match no longer falls seconds behind or drops into "Syncing…" every so often. The game loses a few frames per second but keeps up with the team.
  • Less lag late in long runs and in endless mode: the candies and coins lying on the ground, the moves that aim where the horde gathers (like Fusion Bolt and Future Sight) and big marks on the ground, like the comets’, cost much less. With hundreds of foes on screen, the game now holds more frames per second, on computers and phones.

October 28, 2026

Arena: 4v4 MOBA

  • New mode, Arena: a 4v4 MOBA in the Vale. Defeat wild Pokémon, gather Energy and score it in the rival team’s goals. After 7 minutes, the team with the most points wins.
  • The Stadium has opened south of your home in the lobby, with Ninjask as the judge. The boards on its forecourt lead to the Arena, the ranking, the season, your history and the rules.
  • Four ways to play: a quick match against bots (no account needed), Casual and Ranked in the public queue, or a room with friends for up to 8 players. Bots fill the empty seats.
  • Ranked runs in two-week seasons: climb from Poké Ball to Master Ball, fight for the season’s ranking and earn a title at the end. Season 1 starts on November 2.
  • From Oct 29 on, the Daily Challenge no longer lands on Nightmare: when the draw picks it, the challenge plays on Hard.
All changes (5)

Ranked and the Stadium

  • Casual and Ranked use the public queue and need an account with an @. The queue groups players of a similar level and, if it takes a while, bots fill the match (after 90 s). A Ranked match needs at least 2 trainers to count for points.
  • Each Ranked win gives ranking points, plus a bonus for how you played; every tier has three divisions and you never drop below the tier you reached that season. Leaving mid-match counts as a loss. Matches against bots and with friends go into your history but don’t touch the ranking.
  • A new season starts every two weeks: your points go back to the start of the tier below where you finished, and anyone with at least 5 Ranked matches earns the title of the highest tier they reached.

Arena

  • Patches of tall grass across the Vale: inside one, rivals can’t see or target you, but your automatic attacks stop. A rival coming close, taking or landing a hit and using your special reveal you for a moment.
  • Two teams of 4 in the Vale: two lanes, north and south, with the jungle in between. Each team has three goals: one in each lane and one in the base, which only opens once one of that team’s lane goals falls. Each goal holds a number of points and breaks when they run out.
  • Wild Pokémon drop Energy (you carry up to 30). Stand inside a rival goal to score: the more Energy, the longer it takes, teammates beside you speed it up and a hit from a rival interrupts it. In your own goal you heal and run faster.
  • Knocked out, you drop half your Energy and come back at your base after a few seconds. Drednaw shows up in the middle mid-match and gives a shield and Energy to the team that defeats it.
  • The moves are the same as in regular runs, with level-up picks: the cards show up in a corner and your Pokémon keeps fighting while you choose. Everyone starts even: nothing from the shop or the Poké Mart comes into the Arena.
  • In a match with more than 4 trainers, the ones who joined after the fourth now walk, use their special and pick their cards: they used to stand still the whole match.

Daily Challenge

  • The challenge turns every upgrade and item off so everyone plays the same, and on Nightmare that made the run impossible. From Oct 29 on, a day that would draw Nightmare plays on Hard, with the same Pokémon and the same map; every other day stays the same.

Co-op

  • Slower phones no longer get stuck on "Syncing…" late in a run: a player who falls behind catches up with the team again.
  • Co-op now works on older iPhones and browsers, which couldn’t keep up with the team’s run.

Fixes

  • Runs on a slow or shaky connection no longer miss the ranking for nothing: the game keeps asking until it gets the run’s ranking slot, and if it’s late when you press Play, the loading screen waits a bit longer and offers to play without ranking.
  • When an egg hatches, OK is now the button picked by default, and the screen waits a moment before taking keys: the key that skipped the animation no longer starts a run with the Pokémon by accident.
  • In the cave, the mini map only shows what you have already lit, and the stairs only appear once you have seen them: finding the way is the challenge again.
  • Darkrai’s Nightmare no longer stutters on simpler phones as you reach it, between phases and at the capture: the lights in the dark are much lighter to draw now. The minimap and the on-screen news cost less during a run too.
  • Levitate now also floats over what lingers on the ground: bosses’ trails of fire, ice and lightning, the flames spreading through Ho-Oh’s tower, spitters’ puddles and the world’s rocks.
  • A legend’s card in the select now says only solo wins count toward catching it (co-op wins don’t).
  • Moves that make your Pokémon leap or dash (Flame Charge, Aqua Step, Quick Attack and others) no longer carry you out of a rift’s portal or a place’s entrance like the cave: the dash stops there and you go in. And while you walk, Flame Charge only leaps onto crowds ahead of you, so it doesn’t pull you away from where you want to go (a Poké Ball, say).
  • On the dark theme, the names of moves, items, forms and targets in the Dojo’s panel show up again.
  • Shiny Mega Tyranitar got its own sprite, in its sandy colors: it used to Mega Evolve in the regular ones.
  • In the partner select, many TMs no longer cover the difficulty on phones: the Bag and the TMs scroll sideways.

October 27, 2026

Dojo

  • Machoke’s new Dojo in the lobby, west of your home: train any Pokémon you already have, captured legends included, on targets that don’t hit back, with no horde and no clock.
  • Try moves (TMs and legendary moves too), A/B paths, items, forms and abilities and watch each move’s DPS on the meter.
  • Save the build: in your runs, the cards that follow it wear ★ BUILD.

Legendary rifts and endless mode

  • Winning pays lots of EXP, Poké Dollars, Poké Balls, items and a heal. Sometimes the legendary is shiny and pays double. If time runs out, the rift collapses and throws you back.
  • After beating Mewtwo, keep going in endless mode: the horde keeps growing and Arceus comes hunting at 20:00. It can be beaten, if you are strong enough.
  • Time survived is worth more now: every minute past 15:00 pays extra Poké Dollars at the end of the run.
