How to play

Pokémon Survivors is a Vampire Survivors-style roguelite: you only move, and your Pokémon attacks on its own. This guide explains everything that happens in a run, with the game's numbers.

On a gamepad, the D-pad and left stick move your Pokémon and navigate every menu, A confirms, B goes back and Start pauses. In a run, A fires the special attack.

On phones you can install the game as a fullscreen app: open the browser menu and choose "Add to Home Screen".

The run

Pick one of 27 starters, Kanto to Paldea. Each one has its own stats, starting move, learnset and special attack (see the Pokédex).

Walk around and dodge: your moves fire on their own as their cooldowns come back. Survive 15 minutes of ever bigger hordes. At the end Mewtwo shows up, and beating it wins the run. After that you can keep going in endless mode.

If your HP hits zero the run is over (unless you are holding a Revive, or are in a rift’s arena or a place, which just send you back out to the map). The Poké Dollars you picked up go to your wallet.

Regular enemies get tougher every minute (bosses don't): at 5:00, 2.2x HP and 1.35x damage; at 10:00, 4.6x HP and 1.94x damage; at 15:00, 8.8x HP and 2.89x damage.

Biomes and weather

The map is a patchwork of 9 biomes, Minecraft style: each region is several screens across, and walking takes you out of one and into the next with no loading screen. At the border the ground blends little by little, and entering a new biome shows its name and changes the music. A run always starts on Route 1; the calm biomes lie around it, the cave, the Power Plant and the desert further out, snow, the volcano and the tower far away, and farther still everything mixes. Each run draws where each region lies. Terrain, breakables and points of interest dress for the biome they stand in (pyramids in the desert, a lighthouse on the beach, smokestacks at the Power Plant).

Each biome pulls the horde towards its types: around you, the stage's species with a local type show up 3x more (more Grass and Bug in the forest, more Rock and Ground in the cave), without leaving the others out. Difficulty and stages follow the clock, wherever you are.

The weather changes a few times per run and applies to everyone: your moves and the enemies' attacks alike. It follows the biome you stand in (in co-op, the team lead's), and the weather icon shows next to the clock.

The wild horde

The horde has 415 wild species from all 9 generations, and every run draws its own cast: about 18 species, only a few new ones per stage, so you can tell who is coming.

Each species sits in a tier set by its base stat total. The first stages draw from tier 1 (weak basic Pokémon) and the end of the run from tiers 4 and 5 (evolved Pokémon). Swarms take a flying Pokémon of their tier, and rings a Pokémon of their tier you have already met this run.

In-game stats come from the base stats: HP from the tier and the bulk (HP, Defense and Special Defense), damage from the tier and the best attacking stat, speed from base Speed, EXP from HP, and knockback resistance from HP and Defense (flying and ghost types are lighter).

Once the horde passes 90% of the stage's cap, new arrivals come in as elites (up to 30% of the cap), with a red outline: 1.6x HP and EXP and 1.25x damage. Keeping the screen clear keeps it from turning into a horde of elites. No elites spawn during the Mewtwo fight.

From 1:00, one of each stage's new species runs: 1.6x faster (never faster than you without bonuses), with 70% of the HP, speed lines, and it aims where you're going to be, up to 1s ahead. Running in circles won't shake it.

Levels and moves

Fainted Pokémon drop Exp. Candies. Walk near them to collect them (the Dowsing Machine widens the reach).

On every level up, pick 1 of 3 offers: a new move from your Pokémon's learnset, a move upgrade, a new held item or another item level. You can hold up to 6 moves and 6 items. Moves go up to Lv 8.

Type items (Charcoal, Mystic Water…) only show up if your Pokémon learns moves of that type. When there is nothing left to offer, you get an Oran Berry (+40 HP).

Reroll (draw new offers) and Skip (pass on a level up without picking anything, handy when no offer fits) are charges bought in the Kecleon Shop.

A run starts with a draft: with the clock stopped, you pick your 2nd move out of 3 from your Pokémon’s list.

Every base move splits into two paths at Lv 4 and Lv 7: the card says "2 paths" and, once picked, opens into both. Each path changes how the move plays (bouncing, exploding, leaving a trail, echoing…), not just its numbers.

Every card rolls a rarity: a rare one (10% chance, blue frame) gives 2 levels at once, and a shiny one (1 in 64, twice as often with the Shiny Charm) is a free pick, followed by another.

Forget takes an offer out of the run: the card flies off, the slot is rerolled and that move, item or duo move never comes back. Charges come from the Kecleon Shop’s Escape Rope, every boss beaten and every evolution.

The move list grows with your Pokémon: each evolution unlocks new moves (a toast shows which), and they show up more often for the next 3 level ups. There are more moves than slots, so every run leaves some out.

Beaten bosses drop a TM with a move from outside the learnset, the Move Tutor teaches moves that suit your type, and clearing a rift offers the legendary’s own move (one per run). With every slot full, learning one asks you to forget a move.

Your Pokémon evolves at Lv 12 and Lv 28, just like on the handhelds. Each evolution adds HP and stats, and the final one powers up the special attack.

At its first evolution (Lv 12 for Smeargle, which never evolves), your Pokémon awakens an Ability: pick 1 of 3 (the third is the Hidden Ability), and it lasts the whole run. Eevee, Snorunt, Scyther, Charcadet, Kirlia and Poliwhirl can evolve into more than one form, each with its own type, special, moves and Abilities. The paths that need an item in the official games need it here too (Gallade and Froslass the Dawn Stone, Politoed the King's Rock, Scizor the Metal Coat, Kleavor the Black Augurite, Armarouge and Ceruledge their armors, Espeon, Umbreon, Leafeon, Glaceon and Sylveon their stones): bought at the Kecleon Shop, it's yours for good; dropped by a boss, it lasts until the run ends. Without items, Eevee becomes Vaporeon, Jolteon or Flareon, drawn at random, and with items you choose; Scyther and Charcadet without an item don't evolve, but get 15% stronger. Some single-line evolutions need an item too: Porygon2 the Up-Grade, Porygon-Z the Dubious Disc and Exeggutor the Leaf Stone; without it, the form before stays, 15% stronger.

Special attack

Besides the automatic moves, every Pokémon has a manual special attack: press Space, the A / ✕ gamepad button or the round button on screen. It charges for a moment (you move slower) and then fires: some aim the way you are walking, others hit all around you or fall from the sky on the nearest foes. Quick Attack and Extreme Speed are manual too, since they move you: press Shift, X / □ or the lunge button to lunge at the foe ahead.

Each of the 18 types has its own special, and your Pokémon uses its first type's. Its cooldown shrinks with the Cooldown stat, down to 4s. At the final evolution it becomes the stronger version, with more damage and area.

Some agile lines trade their type special for a chain dash: the Pokémon slashes through to the nearest foe and, on a hit, has half a second to press again and jump to the next one, up to its jump limit. The cooldown only starts when the chain ends. Aegislash trades its special for Stance Change instead: it swaps between Shield Forme, which takes less damage, and Blade Forme, which hits harder.

Bosses

Bosses arrive at fixed times. Legendaries make an entrance with a screen flash, their cry and a banner. All of them get an HP bar at the top and their own attacks: marked circles on the ground show where a hit will land, so step out of them.

Each boss switches between two ways of fighting every 4 to 9 seconds: up close, it rushes in and slams the area around itself; at range, it stops a few steps away and aims at you. Under 50% HP it enrages (red outline, cry and a screen flash): 15% faster, 20% less time between attacks and, 35% of the time, a second attack right after the first.

Standing still doesn't work: every boss aims where you stand or along the path you're taking, and running far away earns you a leap or an aimed volley. Enraging also unlocks a new attack. On Hard bosses attack 8% more often, with 15% more projectiles and slightly shorter warnings, and chain 50% of the time when enraged; on Nightmare, 16% more often, 25% more projectiles, 65% chains and one extra attack each. The legendaries on the map (the trio, the mythical, Mewtwo and Arceus) fight in three phases, ticked on the HP bar: each phase brings a new way to move (flying with a shadow, charges, teleports, cages) and the last one an ultimate with a marked safe spot (shelters, the gap between lances, a ring's way out, far from or right next to the boss). From Hard up, Mewtwo's last phase also leaps and fires wide volleys.

Bosses don't get tougher over time, and each one drops a Poké Ball and a shower of Poké Dollars.

For each boss's first 60s, while it's nearby, only 30% of the regular enemies spawn, so the fight is with it; then the horde comes back. Each boss has until the next one arrives: if it's still standing when the next boss shows up, it flees and leaves nothing behind. Mewtwo is the last one: it blasts the horde away when it arrives and, while it stands, only 20% of the regular enemies spawn, so the fight is about dodging its attacks.

At 7:00, 9:30 and 12:00 come the three members of a legendary trio, drawn at the start of each run: Kanto birds (Articuno, Zapdos, Moltres), Johto beasts (Raikou, Entei, Suicune) and Hoenn Regis (Regirock, Regice, Registeel). HP, contact damage and speed belong to the time slot, not the Pokémon, so no draw is easier than another; what changes is the attacks.

At the end, with a 10% chance, a mythical Pokémon joins Mewtwo: Mew, Celebi or Jirachi, also drawn per run.

Special enemies

Some wild Pokémon do more than chase: they heal, shield and call in the horde, or attack from afar. They are seasoning, never the bulk: from 1:00 on, each stage brings at most one species with a behavior, it shows up less than the rest and there are never more than 6 alive at once. Keep an eye out.

Every one of their attacks warns you first: a "!" over the head, a red circle, a line or a shadow on the ground that blinks faster as the hit gets close. The first time each one shows up in a run, a message explains what it does.

Legendary rifts and endless mode

Every run has 2 legendary rifts: one around 4:00 and another around 10:20. Each one starts with an omen: the legendary's weather takes over the sky (pouring rain, harsh sun, a gale, a storm, a rainbow, a meteor shower, time stumbling) and a huge tear opens in the air off screen, with a purple arrow on the edge pointing at it. It stays open for 40s. If a boss is alive when it's due, the rift waits up to 45s for the boss to fall, so both rifts come even when bosses take a while.

Stepping in is optional: stand inside the tear for 1s. Each legendary has its own domain (table below), and first comes the crossing: a place bigger than the screen, heading north, with the Pokémon that serve the legendary coming at you and a threat closing in from behind (the tide, a wall of lava, floors crumbling away, time coming apart) that hurts whoever lags. At the end, the way back seals, the camera travels to the legendary and it arrives with a title card and its own theme.

The battle has three phases, marked on the HP bar. Each phase transforms the arena (the cavern floods into islands, lava rises in channels, the top of the Sky Pillar falls into the sky) and builds to an ultimate, always telegraphed and with a marked safe spot: behind a rock, on an island, in the gap between beams, under Ho-Oh's wings. Standing still doesn't work: attacks land where you stand. Latias and Latios fight together, and Deoxys swaps Formes every phase. If the battle runs past about 3 minutes, the domain collapses and throws you back with no reward.

Once beaten, the legendary kneels and you throw the Poké Ball: every win counts toward catching it (see Legend Mode). Fainting inside doesn't end the run: the rift casts you back onto the map with 30% HP, no reward, and your Revive stays in the Bag. The run clock stops inside; the horde outside freezes and nothing new spawns until you come back.

The reward is worth the risk: lots of EXP, a bag of ₽ on top of the boss shower, extra Poké Balls, a Rare Candy, a Super Potion and half your HP back. In 1 of 8 rifts the legendary is shiny and pays double ₽ plus another Poké Ball.

Endless mode

After you beat Mewtwo, the victory screen offers to keep going in endless mode. If you don't want to, you leave with the regular victory, and the bonus is paid right away.

The horde keeps growing: more enemies spawn, the on-screen cap rises and rings and swarms arrive every 40 seconds. Rifts keep opening.

At 20:00, Arceus descends to hunt you. It gets faster the longer it hunts, until nobody can outrun it, and it never loses you: wander off and it reappears at the edge of the screen. It can be beaten, but it has 300,000 HP; if it falls, it comes back 2 minutes later, stronger.

Time survived is the main score: every minute past 15:00 pays ₽60 in the bonus, and every defeated Arceus another ₽2,500.

Legend Mode

All 23 legendaries in the game can be caught and played in Legend Mode. To catch one, beat it as a boss, in any mode: every win before the catch, it breaks free of the Poké Ball and the game keeps count (Kyogre broke free… 2/3); on the win that completes it, it's caught and shows up in the Legendaries tab of the partner select. Beating a shiny one on the catching win, or on any win after, unlocks its shiny.

In Legend Mode the legendary doesn't evolve: it awakens at Lv 12 and Lv 28 (an Ability, power and a Forget charge; Kyogre and Groudon turn Primal, Rayquaza turns Mega), starts with its legendary move and an innate ability that's always on, and the horde comes tougher (Pressure), all evolved Pokémon, with no basic forms and no outbreak of the day. Latias, Latios and Mewtwo Mega Evolve with the Mega Stone and the MEGA button, like everyone else, and Deoxys's special swaps its Forme (Normal, Attack, Defense, Speed). A run lasts as long as ever, all three difficulties still apply and the mode has its own ranking, split by difficulty: on the rankings, the Standard | Legendary row switches boards.

The legendaries on the map (the run’s trio, the rare mythical beside Mewtwo, Mewtwo at the end and Arceus in endless mode) make an entrance with a title card and fight in three phases, building to an ultimate with a marked safe spot. When one falls, the capture plays right there in a quick scene; rift legendaries are caught at the end of their domain. A legendary never faces itself: the rifts, the trio and the mythical skip the one you play, and a Mewtwo takes on the Mewtwo Clone at the end.

Online co-op

In the lobby, Co-op opens team play: up to 4 trainers in the same run, each with their own Pokémon. Create a room and bring your friends with the link (opening it goes straight into the room), the 5-character code or your Friends list, which shows who's online, in a room or playing, and sends the invite right away. Everyone picks their Pokémon and gets ready, and the host picks the difficulty and starts. Co-op uses your account: the team sees your @name, and the ₽ you earn is saved to it.

EXP belongs to the team: every Exp. Candy reaches everyone, so the whole team levels up together and picks at the same time (past 15s, the game picks for you). One player's evolution, Ability, TM or Poké Ball doesn't stop the others' run, and bosses and rifts drop each player their own TM, Poké Ball, Alpha prize and Mega Stone.

Whoever faints turns into a Poké Ball on the ground: a teammate standing next to it for 3s revives them with 40% HP. The run ends when nobody can carry on; after Mewtwo, the team has 20s to vote on going into endless mode. If your connection drops, the game reconnects and you pick up where you left off. At the end, the team's ₽ is split among the players who started the run.

For now co-op plays the standard mode: places (the cave, the graveyard and the sky) don't open, landmarks are scenery only, rift legendaries fight in the old arena instead of their domain, and Legend Mode, the Bag, the shop's TMs, evolution items and Bottle Caps, the daily Outbreak, Halloween Night and the solo Daily Challenge stay out. Co-op has its own rankings, for Duos, Trios and Squads (teams of 2, 3 and 4), and the Daily co-op challenge: the same seed and difficulty for every team, any Pokémon, no Poké Mart upgrades, unlimited tries (the team's best run counts) and no ribbons.

Places: the cave, the graveyard and the sky

From 1:40, about every 4.17 minutes, the entrance to a place shows up off screen, with an arrow on the edge pointing at it: a cave’s mouth in a hill, a graveyard’s gate or an updraft between standing stones. It stays open for 45s and, if a boss is alive, waits up to 45s for it to fall. A run shows different places before any repeats.

Going in is optional: stand 0.6s in the doorway. Inside, the world waits, as in a rift: the horde freezes and nothing new spawns, but the run clock keeps going. Each place has its own Pokémon, which get stronger as the run goes on, and a time limit; when it runs out, you come back out the way you went in. Fainting inside doesn’t end the run: a rescue team takes you back to the door with 30% of your HP, without the reward and with your Revive kept.

Hidden zones

Not every place comes with an arrow. In about 45% of runs, the way into a hidden zone shows up unannounced, just ahead of where you’re heading, dressed as scenery: a gap in a thicket where the leaves rustle, or a cracked rock that glints now and then. It stays for 80s and isn’t on the minimap until someone walks up to it.

Inside live Pokémon worth far more EXP than the horde, and now and then something sparkles where they hide. Finding each zone the first time brings home a look no shop sells. The rest is up to you.

Landmarks on the map

The map has landmarks, placed from the run's seed like mountains and lakes: at most one per 3×3 stretch of the map (80% of them have one), always dressed for the biome the run is in. As you come close, a wooden sign names it in the corner of the screen.

Four of them keep a treasure: stand next to it for 0.6s to open it. Each opens once per run, and a glint marks the ones still closed. The tunnel has no treasure but it's a shortcut: stand in one of its mouths for 0.4s and you come out of the other, on the far side of the ridge, while the horde loses track of you.

Between them, bushes, fallen logs and lone rocks and trees. All of it blocks whoever walks (the horde too), but flying Pokémon, ghosts and bosses go over it.

Each run also draws a kind of map, which changes what the terrain holds: Archipelago (lakes and channels everywhere), Canyon (rock walls and gorges with tight passes), Deep Forest (walls of trees forming trails), Ruins Field (columns that block enemy shots), Open Plains or the Classic Route. The sign at the start of the run names the map and the types it suits. The terrain suits your Pokémon: Water surfs the lakes faster than it walks, Flying (and Levitate) never gets slowed in water nor slides on ice, Ice skates on ice without sliding and Ghost slips through trees, bushes, rocks, logs, columns and hedges (mountains, rock walls, towers and walls still stop it).

Shinies

Every regular wild Pokémon has a 1 in 450 chance to spawn shiny. It becomes an elite with 8x HP and 10x EXP, and always drops a Poké Ball and Poké Dollars. Map bosses, Mewtwo and Arceus are never shiny, but a rift's legendary has a 1 in 8 chance to be, and pays more.

Your starter has a 1 in 64 chance to be shiny, and stays shiny through every evolution of that run.

A Poké Ball (from bosses and shinies) grants a random upgrade on the spot, or a move evolution if one is ready.

A defeated shiny is yours: it shows up as caught, sparkle and all, in the in-game Pokédex (Pokédex, on the title menu). The Pokémon that comes out of an egg counts as caught too (and its evolution line as seen), and so does every form you played, the ones you evolved into included. The Pokédex holds the game's 590 species, remembers every one you have seen and how many you defeated, and the end of a run shows what is new.

Eggs and the incubator

Besides the 27 starters, 63 playable Pokémon families hatch from eggs, from every type and generation: 90 Pokémon to play in all. Each one has its own starting move, special, mid-run evolution and stats.

Bosses, locked chests and rifts sometimes leave an egg, at most 2 per run. It goes to the incubator, which has 3 slots. Every 16 px walked in a run counts as a step, and every egg in the incubator walks along. An egg found mid-run already counts the steps taken after it.

The Incubator tab at Kecleon's shop sells more slots, yours for good: slot 4 for ₽150,000 (with the shop at Tent (Lv 2)) and slot 5 for ₽500,000 (with the shop at Kecleon Mart (Lv 4)), up to 5 slots. It also sells the Quick Incubator (₽8,000): in the incubator, tap an egg to use it, and that egg walks 2x as fast until it hatches.

Once an egg is ready, it hatches when you leave the run or in the incubator (in the main menu and in the Eggs tab of the Pokémon select). Families you don't have yet come out 3x more often. The hatchling has a 1 in 100 chance to be shiny, and then it stays shiny in every run. Guarantee: after 5 eggs of a kind with no new Pokémon, the next one is sure to bring one (the incubator shows the count).

A duplicate, or an egg found with the incubator full, becomes Poké Dollars. A player who never had an egg is sure to get the first one.

The Friendship Egg never drops in runs: it comes as a Mystery Gift when you sign up through someone's invite, and when a friend you invited finishes 3 runs. Only Munchlax hatches from it. At 3 friends you get the Ambassador title and an exclusive frame, at 5 an Ultra rare egg, and at 10 a shiny Dratini. Your link and each friend's progress are on the Invite friends card, on the home screen.

The Haunted Egg never drops in runs either: only Duskull's stall sells it, at the plaza, during Halloween Night (Oct 24 to Nov 2), one a day. Only Litwick, Phantump, Drifloon and Sandygast come out of it, 25% each, owned or not. After the event, it's back next year.

The Kecleon Egg never drops in runs either: it's a fixed card in the shop's Showcase: the day's first costs ₽10,000 and each one after costs double the last, up to 6 a day (₽320,000 for the sixth). Only Porygon, Exeggcute, Scraggy and Smeargle come out of it, 25% each. Porygon2 needs the Up-Grade, Porygon-Z the Dubious Disc and Exeggutor the Leaf Stone; Smeargle never evolves, but its Sketch copies the nearest foe's move. 100 stamps on the loyalty card bring a Kecleon Egg sure to hatch shiny.

Kecleon Shop and Poké Dollars

Fainted enemies sometimes drop Poké Dollars: a coin, a stack or a bag (₽10, ₽50 and ₽250). Tougher ones drop them more often, and bosses and shinies always pay out.

A bonus comes at the end of the run: ₽30 per minute survived, ₽1 per 4 fainted foes and ₽1,000 for winning. Quitting from the pause menu keeps the coins but forfeits the bonus.

Spend it all in the Kecleon Shop, on the plaza east of your partner's home in the lobby. Today's Showcase is the same for everyone and turns over at midnight Brasília time: a free gift every day, the Kecleon Egg (up to 6 a day, each for double the last), a deal at 30% off, consumables for your Bag, the day's egg and a style. Kecleon's purple brother sells TMs (buy one once, arm it before a run and the first TM that drops is that one) and evolution items: a path that needs an item only opens with it (no Dawn Stone, no Gallade), and a boss may also drop one in a run, for that run only. Training holds the permanent upgrades: the Refund button gives back everything you spent on them, so you can try other builds. It also sells Bottle Caps: +3% to everything per level, up to 3, only for the Pokémon that gets them. In Expansion you invest to grow the shop (Cart, Tent, Shop, Kecleon Mart and Department Store), with more items in the Showcase and the Bag. The Incubator tab sells extra incubator slots (for good) and the Quick Incubator, which makes one egg walk twice as fast. Friday to Sunday, from the Kecleon Mart on, Meowth's caravan stops on the plaza with an ultra egg, the Gold Bottle Cap and exclusive TMs; with 7 stamps on your loyalty card (one per day you visit the shop) he gives you 20% off. Without an account, progress is saved only in this browser.

Plaza Festival: about once a month, for 14 days, the fountain on the Kecleon plaza becomes a community pot. With an account (with a @handle and 3 finished runs), you toss ₽ into the fountain, from ₽100 up to ₽200k a day. Every milestone the pot passes becomes a Gift for every player, even guests and those who didn't give, and waits in the gift box until 7 days after the end. Giving ₽50k in one festival earns the Patron title, and the 10 biggest donors show on the fountain's plaque (you can hide your name). The first is the Lantern Festival, from Nov 3 to 16.

Missions and achievements

Missions is in the title screen menu. Every day brings 3 daily missions (₽100–₽300) and every week 2 weekly ones (₽1,000–₽3,000). They are drawn from your device's date, so everyone gets the same ones on the same day: dailies change at midnight and weeklies on Monday.

Progress counts at the end of every run, even if you quit from the pause menu, and adds up the runs of the day or week. Survival, level, endless and species missions count your best run. Missions use what the game has: defeat Pokémon of a type, in a weather or in a biome, win rifts, open chests, grab Meowth's Pay Day, defeat shinies and bosses, collect Poké Dollars, walk, see different species, hatch eggs (it counts when the egg hatches) and beat Mewtwo with a starter of some type. The reward goes straight to your wallet, and some weeklies also give an egg.

Achievements are lifetime goals with a bronze, silver or gold medal (Bronze ₽100, Silver ₽500, Gold ₽2,000 + Rare Egg). Some grant a title, like Shiny Hunter: tap it on the Achievements tab to show it on the title screen and on every run's results. Reward eggs go to the incubator (when it's full they become Poké Dollars) and, at the end of a run, share the per-run egg limit with the eggs you found.
